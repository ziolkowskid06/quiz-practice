  - id: 1
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "Case: Northshore Engineering Services installs and maintains rotating equipment for industrial customers. Its project revenue is stable but margins are falling. Software-first entrants offer remote monitoring, and several customers ask Northshore to reduce unexpected failures rather than merely complete repair work. Before deciding whether to build a subscription service, what is the best first tool?"
    options:
      - "Porter's Five Forces"
      - "Balanced Scorecard"
      - "Kotter's Eight Phases of Change"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
    answer: "Porter's Five Forces"
    explanation: "The initial issue is the changing structure and profitability of the industry. Five Forces helps Northshore assess rivalry, buyer power, supplier power, substitutes, and new entrants before deciding how its competitive position is changing. A scorecard and change programme come after strategic choices are clearer."

  - id: 2
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "Case continuation: Northshore learns that buyers can compare providers easily, customers are concentrated, and software vendors offer credible substitutes for some site visits. However, Northshore has trusted relationships, deep asset knowledge, field data, and strong safety credibility. What is the best next tool to synthesize this internal and external diagnosis into strategic options?"
    options:
      - "SWOT and TOWS"
      - "McKinsey 7-S Framework"
      - "Lewin's Force Field Analysis"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
    answer: "SWOT and TOWS"
    explanation: "SWOT combines the external findings, such as buyer power and substitutes, with Northshore's internal strengths and weaknesses. TOWS then helps create options, for example using trusted relationships and asset expertise to address the opportunity for outcome-focused maintenance services."

  - id: 3
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "Case continuation: The leadership team wants to understand where value is created and lost from data capture through diagnosis, field response, customer reporting, renewal, and supplier partnerships. It also wants to identify which activities could become a defensible advantage. Which tool is the best fit?"
    options:
      - "Porter's Value Chain and Value System"
      - "Ansoff's Growth Grid"
      - "Kotter's Eight Phases of Change"
      - "Stacey's Strategy from Complexity"
    answer: "Porter's Value Chain and Value System"
    explanation: "The Value Chain identifies how internal primary and support activities create cost or differentiation. The wider Value System extends the analysis to suppliers, partners, channels, and customers. It is the best tool for locating the operational sources of value, friction, and advantage."

  - id: 5
    type: multiple-choice

    answer: "De Wit and Meyer's Strategy Tensions"
    explanation: "The company faces tensions such as short-term profitability versus long-term strategic investment, control versus experimentation, and responsiveness to individual service requests versus standardized scalable processes. De Wit and Meyer makes these trade-offs explicit so leaders can choose, sequence, separate, or synthesize a response."

    type: multiple-choice
    question: "Case continuation: The team does not yet know whether customers will pay for the subscription, whether sensor data will be reliable enough, or whether remote insights will reduce failures.     options:
      - "Apply a standard operating procedure to all customers immediately"
      - "Run safe-to-learn experiments and adapt from evidence"
      - "Wait until certainty and full agreement exist before acting"
      - "Treat the uncertainty as a reason to abandon the opportunity"
    answer: "Run safe-to-learn experiments and adapt from evidence"
    explanation: "The opportunity sits in a complex zone: certainty is low and relevant stakeholders may disagree about the problem and solution. Northshore should use bounded experiments, explicit learning goals, feedback, and adaptation rather than prematurely standardizing or waiting for impossible certainty."

  - id: 7
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "Case continuation: Northshore needs a disciplined decision after the pilot. It wants to combine evidence of customer value and willingness to pay with an assessment of the cash available to absorb the risk of scaling. Which tool is the best fit?"
    options:
      - "Burgelman and Grove's Strategy Bet Model"
      - "Balanced Scorecard"
      - "Lewin's Force Field Analysis"
      - "McKinsey 7-S Framework"
    answer: "Burgelman and Grove's Strategy Bet Model"
    explanation: "The Strategy Bet Model evaluates two core questions: how validated the opportunity is and whether the organization has sufficient cash resilience. It helps distinguish safe, strategic, foolish, and dead bets, and supports a deliberate scale, pivot, partner, pause, or stop decision."
