    question: "A company wants to translate its strategy into linked objectives, measures, targets, initiatives, and owners across financial results, customer value, internal processes, and organizational capability. Which tool is the best fit?"
    options:
      - "Kaplan and Norton's Balanced Scorecard"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"

    answer: "Kaplan and Norton's Balanced Scorecard"

  - id: 2
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A product team has missed its renewal target. It has already improved onboarding, training, sales follow-up, and reporting several times. It now needs to test whether its assumptions about the customer problem, buyer, value proposition, and revenue model are wrong. Which tool should it use?"
    options:
      - "Argyris's Double-Loop Learning"
      - "Balanced Scorecard"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
      - "Kotter's Eight Phases of Change"
    answer: "Argyris's Double-Loop Learning"
    explanation: "Double-loop learning questions governing variables such as assumptions, goals, policies, incentives, and strategic rules after a persistent mismatch. A Balanced Scorecard would show the missed target, but Argyris guides the deeper inquiry into why the target and the model may be wrong."

  - id: 3
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A company has evidence that a new service creates customer value and has enough cash to fund growth. It needs to decide how to move from pilot to controlled scale-up while continuing to monitor validation and downside risk. Which tool is the best fit?"
    options:
      - "Burgelman and Grove's Strategy Bet Model"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
      - "SWOT Analysis"
      - "Cummings and Wilson: Orientation and Animation"
    answer: "Burgelman and Grove's Strategy Bet Model"
    explanation: "The Strategy Bet Model links opportunity validation and cash reserves to commitment choices. A validated opportunity with sufficient cash is a Safe Bet and should scale in stages. BCG may classify a portfolio unit, but it does not explicitly test validation and cash resilience."

  - id: 4
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A leadership team wants one visual structure that links a strategic ambition to a few priorities, required capabilities, measures, governance, milestones, and learning loops. Which tool is the best fit?"
    options:
      - "House of Strategy"
      - "Balanced Scorecard"
      - "Scenario Planning"
      - "Porter's Generic Strategies"
    answer: "House of Strategy"
    explanation: "The House of Strategy provides an overall architecture: roof for ambition, pillars for priorities, foundation for capabilities and operating model, and footings for measures, governance, risk controls, and learning. The Balanced Scorecard is a strong measurement system within the footings, but it is not the full architecture."

  - id: 5
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A company needs to track whether its product strategy is becoming real before annual revenue results arrive. Which measure is the best example of a leading indicator?"
    options:
      - "Annual recurring revenue"
      - "Gross margin at year end"
      - "Percentage of pilot customers activated and using the service"
      - "Last year's market share"
    answer: "Percentage of pilot customers activated and using the service"
    explanation: "Activation and product use are leading indicators because they signal whether future customer value, renewal, and revenue may occur. Annual recurring revenue, margin, and historical market share are lagging outcomes that appear after earlier capability, process, and customer signals."

  - id: 6
    type: multiple-choice

    options:
      - "Mintzberg's Deliberate and Emergent Strategy"
      - "Argyris's Double-Loop Learning"
      - "Hrebiniak's Model of Strategy Execution"
      - "Porter's Value Chain and Value System"
    answer: "Mintzberg's Deliberate and Emergent Strategy"
    explanation: "Mintzberg explains how a useful pattern discovered through action can become emergent strategy and shape realized strategy. Double-loop learning may help challenge assumptions, but Mintzberg specifically distinguishes intended, deliberate, unrealized, emergent, and realized strategy."

    options:

      - "Declare the product a Dog without considering market growth or share"
