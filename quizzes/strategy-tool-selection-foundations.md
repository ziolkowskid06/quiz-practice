questions:
  - id: 1
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A regional engineering firm wants to understand whether its profit pressure comes mainly from powerful buyers, scarce suppliers, substitute solutions, new entrants, or existing rivals. Which tool should it use first?"
    options:
      - "SWOT Analysis"
      - "Porter's Five Forces"
      - "Ansoff's Growth Grid"
      - "Balanced Scorecard"
    answer: "Porter's Five Forces"
    explanation: "Porter's Five Forces diagnoses structural industry pressure from rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, and supplier power. SWOT can summarize the resulting insight, but it does not analyze these five forces in a structured way."

  - id: 2
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A company wants a concise picture of its internal strengths and weaknesses alongside external opportunities and threats before beginning strategic discussion. Which tool is the best starting point?"
    options:
      - "SWOT Analysis"
      - "Porter's Generic Strategies"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
      - "Kotter's Eight Phases of Change"
    answer: "SWOT Analysis"
    explanation: "SWOT creates a broad current-state diagnosis by separating internal strengths and weaknesses from external opportunities and threats. Porter's Generic Strategies is used later to choose how to compete."

  - id: 3
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "An engineering firm has decided it will enter a market, but it must choose whether to compete through lowest delivered cost, broad differentiation, cost focus, or differentiation focus. Which tool should it use?"
    options:
      - "Porter's Generic Strategies"
      - "Porter's Five Forces"
      - "Kim and Mauborgne's Four Actions Framework"
      - "Scenario Planning"
    answer: "Porter's Generic Strategies"
    explanation: "Porter's Generic Strategies helps choose the basis of competitive advantage and scope: cost leadership, differentiation, cost focus, or differentiation focus. Five Forces diagnoses industry pressure; it does not select the firm's competitive position."

  - id: 4
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A new digital maintenance service is attractive, but it requires a subscription revenue model, product management, customer success, data governance, and processes that do not fit the company's existing project-based business. Which tool is the best fit?"
    options:
      - "Johnson's White Space Model"
      - "Ansoff's Growth Grid"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
      - "Lewin's Force Field Analysis"
    answer: "Johnson's White Space Model"
    explanation: "Johnson's White Space Model is for opportunities that the current business model cannot serve well. It tests whether a new customer value proposition, profit formula, resources, and processes are needed. Ansoff may classify growth, but White Space diagnoses business-model incompatibility."

  - id: 5
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A firm has several product and service lines and wants to decide which should receive investment, which should generate cash, which need an invest-or-exit decision, and which should be harvested or discontinued. Which tool should it use?"
    options:
      - "Ansoff's Growth Grid"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
      - "Porter's Value Chain"
      - "Nonaka and Takeuchi's Knowledge Spiral"
    answer: "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
    explanation: "The BCG Growth-Share Matrix is a portfolio tool. It uses market growth and relative market share to classify units as Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, or Dogs and guide resource allocation. Ansoff is about potential product-market growth routes."

  - id: 6
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A product team keeps receiving reports that experienced field engineers can diagnose faults using subtle contextual clues, but the reasoning stays in individuals' heads and cannot be reused or taught. Which tool should it use?"
    options:
      - "Nonaka and Takeuchi's Knowledge Spiral"
      - "Peters, Athos and Waterman's 7-S Framework"
      - "Argyris's Double-Loop Learning"
      - "Greiner's Growth and Crisis Model"
    answer: "Nonaka and Takeuchi's Knowledge Spiral"
    explanation: "The Knowledge Spiral, or SECI model, helps convert tacit expert knowledge into shared explicit knowledge and back into practical capability through socialization, externalization, combination, and internalization."

  - id: 7
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A company has a clear new strategy, but its structure, systems, skills, staffing, leadership behavior, and shared values still reinforce the old way of working. Which tool should it use to diagnose the misalignment?"
    options:
      - "Peters, Athos and Waterman's 7-S Framework"
      - "Kotter's Eight Phases of Change"
      - "Porter's Value Chain"
      - "Scenario Planning"
    answer: "Peters, Athos and Waterman's 7-S Framework"
    explanation: "The 7-S Framework tests alignment among Strategy, Structure, Systems, Skills, Staff, Style, and Shared Values. Kotter provides a broader sequence for leading a change programme, while 7-S pinpoints internal alignment gaps."

  - id: 8
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A firm faces uncertain customer adoption and uncertain regulation. It wants to explore several plausible futures, identify actions that work across them, and monitor early warning signals. Which tool should it use?"
    options:
      - "Scenario Planning"
      - "Stacey's Strategy from Complexity"
      - "SWOT Analysis"
      - "Hrebiniak's Model of Strategy Execution"
    answer: "Scenario Planning"
    explanation: "Scenario Planning creates multiple plausible futures from critical uncertainties, then identifies robust actions, options, contingent commitments, and signposts. Stacey's model instead helps choose a management approach based on certainty and stakeholder agreement."

  - id: 9
    type: select-all
    question: "Which outputs should a well-run Scenario Planning exercise produce?"
    options:
      - "Several plausible future scenarios"
      - "A single certain forecast"
      - "No-regret actions"
      - "Early-warning signposts"
      - "A permanent strategy that never needs review"
    answers:
      - "Several plausible future scenarios"
      - "No-regret actions"
      - "Early-warning signposts"
    explanation: "Scenario Planning does not claim to predict one certain future. It creates plausible scenarios, identifies actions that work across them, and defines signposts that indicate which future may be emerging."

  - id: 10
    type: select-all
    question: "Which situations are especially suitable for Porter's Value Chain and Value System analysis?"
    options:
      - "Finding activities that create customer value or cost"
      - "Mapping how supplier and customer activities affect the offer"
      - "Selecting the best of four possible futures"
      - "Identifying important linkages between operations, sales, and service"
      - "Finding where a firm can improve differentiation or efficiency"
    answers:
      - "Finding activities that create customer value or cost"
      - "Mapping how supplier and customer activities affect the offer"
      - "Identifying important linkages between operations, sales, and service"
      - "Finding where a firm can improve differentiation or efficiency"
    explanation: "Value Chain analysis maps activities that create cost and value inside the firm, while the Value System extends the view to suppliers, channels, partners, and customers. Scenario Planning is the tool for exploring alternative futures."

  - id: 11
    type: select-all
    question: "Which are the four actions in Kim and Mauborgne's Four Actions Framework?"
    options:
      - "Eliminate"
      - "Reduce"
      - "Raise"
      - "Create"
      - "Coordinate"
      - "Delegate"
    answers:
      - "Eliminate"
      - "Reduce"
      - "Raise"
      - "Create"
    explanation: "Kim and Mauborgne's ERRC Grid asks what factors to Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, and Create. It is used to redesign a value curve rather than simply add features."

  - id: 12
    type: select-all
    question: "Which issues are especially suitable for Hrebiniak's Model of Strategy Execution?"
    options:
      - "Conflicting incentives that reward the old strategy"
      - "Unclear cross-functional decision rights"
      - "Choosing two critical uncertainties for a scenario matrix"
      - "Poor coordination between sales, service, and product teams"
      - "Weak controls and feedback for a strategic initiative"
    answers:
      - "Conflicting incentives that reward the old strategy"
      - "Unclear cross-functional decision rights"
      - "Poor coordination between sales, service, and product teams"
      - "Weak controls and feedback for a strategic initiative"
    explanation: "Hrebiniak focuses on strategy execution through structure, coordination, accountability, incentives, controls, information, culture, leadership, and change. Scenario Planning is better for selecting uncertainties and constructing futures."

  - id: 13
    type: fill-blank
    question: "The four stages of Nonaka and Takeuchi's knowledge spiral are Socialization, Externalization, Combination, and ___."
    answer: "Internalization"
    accept:
      - "internalisation"
    explanation: "SECI stands for Socialization, Externalization, Combination, and Internalization. Internalization is where explicit knowledge is learned through practice until it becomes practical capability."

  - id: 14
    type: fill-blank
    question: "In Ansoff's Growth Grid, selling a new product or service to an existing customer market is called ___."
    answer: "Product development"
    accept:
      - "product-development"
    explanation: "Ansoff's four routes are market penetration, product development, market development, and diversification. Product development means new offerings for markets the organization already knows."

  - id: 15
    type: fill-blank
    question: "In Argyris's learning model, questioning and revising the assumptions, rules, goals, or incentives behind repeated poor results is ___-loop learning."
    answer: "double"
    accept:
      - "double loop"
      - "double-loop"
    explanation: "Double-loop learning examines governing variables such as assumptions, targets, policies, and incentives. Single-loop learning improves actions while keeping those deeper rules intact."

  - id: 16
    type: fill-blank
    question: "In Lewin's Force Field Analysis, forces that hold the current state in place are called ___ forces."
    answer: "restraining"
    accept:
      - "restraint"
      - "restrain"
    explanation: "Lewin distinguishes driving forces, which push toward change, from restraining forces, which hold the status quo in place. Effective change often requires reducing major restraints rather than only increasing pressure."
