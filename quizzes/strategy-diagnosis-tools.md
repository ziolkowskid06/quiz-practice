questions:
  - id: 1
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A company wants a broad first-pass diagnosis that separates factors it can influence from factors in the external environment. Which tool is the best fit?"
    options:
      - "SWOT Analysis"
      - "Porter's Five Forces"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
      - "Scenario Planning"
    answer: "SWOT Analysis"
    explanation: "SWOT separates internal Strengths and Weaknesses from external Opportunities and Threats. Five Forces is narrower because it analyzes industry structure, while BCG analyzes a portfolio and Scenario Planning explores future uncertainty."

  - id: 2
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "An engineering business needs to identify which activities across procurement, operations, sales, service, and partners create customer value, cost, delay, or differentiation. Which tool should it use?"
    options:
      - "Porter's Value Chain and Value System"
      - "Hammer and Champy's Business Process Redesign"
      - "Porter's Generic Strategies"
      - "Kotter's Eight Phases of Change"
    answer: "Porter's Value Chain and Value System"
    explanation: "Value Chain analysis maps activities that create cost and value, while the Value System extends the view to suppliers, channels, partners, and customers. Business Process Redesign is used later when a process needs radical redesign."

  - id: 3
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A firm wants to assess whether its historical data, trusted customer relationships, engineering routines, and specialist expertise could form a hard-to-copy source of advantage. Which tool is the best fit?"
    options:
      - "Core Competencies and Resource-Based View"
      - "Peters, Athos and Waterman's 7-S Framework"
      - "Porter's Five Forces"
      - "Lewin's Force Field Analysis"
    answer: "Core Competencies and Resource-Based View"
    explanation: "Core Competencies and RBV assess internal resources and collective capabilities that may create distinctive advantage. The 7-S Framework assesses organizational alignment, Five Forces assesses industry pressure, and Lewin maps change forces."

  - id: 4
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A senior team suspects that its company is continuing to follow established industry rules while new entrants are changing the basis of competition. Which tool best diagnoses this strategic dynamic?"
    options:
      - "Burgelman's Strategy Dynamics Model"
      - "Burgelman and Grove's Strategy Bet Model"
      - "Michaud and Thoenig's Strategic Orientation"
      - "Mintzberg's Deliberate and Emergent Strategy"
    answer: "Burgelman's Strategy Dynamics Model"
    explanation: "Burgelman's Strategy Dynamics Model compares rule-abiding and rule-changing behavior by the company and its environment. A rule-abiding company in a rule-changing environment is in independent industry change. The Strategy Bet Model instead assesses validation and cash reserves."

  - id: 5
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A business has profitable mature services, a fast-growing high-share service line, a new low-share growth opportunity, and a declining low-share legacy offer. Which tool should it use to classify and prioritize the portfolio?"
    options:
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
      - "Ansoff's Growth Grid"
      - "SWOT Analysis"
      - "House of Strategy"
    answer: "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
    explanation: "The BCG Growth-Share Matrix classifies portfolio units using market growth and relative market share as Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, and Dogs. Ansoff identifies potential product-market growth routes rather than current portfolio allocation."

  - id: 6
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A company is unsure whether a product decision is simple, complicated, complex, or chaotic because stakeholders disagree and the effect of actions is uncertain. Which tool should it use?"
    options:
      - "Stacey's Strategy from Complexity"
      - "Scenario Planning"
      - "De Wit and Meyer's Strategy Tensions"
      - "Balanced Scorecard"
    answer: "Stacey's Strategy from Complexity"
    explanation: "Stacey's model uses certainty about cause and effect plus stakeholder agreement to choose an appropriate management approach: standardize, analyze, experiment, or stabilize. Scenario Planning explores future worlds rather than diagnosing problem complexity."

  - id: 7
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "Leaders say their market faces major disruption, but budgets, hiring, technology decisions, and leadership language show a preference for quick external fixes rather than long-term capability building. Which tool best describes this organizational posture?"
    options:
      - "Michaud and Thoenig's Strategic Orientation"
      - "Burgelman's Strategy Dynamics Model"
      - "Lewin's Force Field Analysis"
      - "Greiner's Growth and Crisis Model"
    answer: "Michaud and Thoenig's Strategic Orientation"
    explanation: "Michaud and Thoenig's model combines perceived pressure to change with response horizon. Strong pressure plus a short-term externally sourced response is a mercenary orientation."

  - id: 8
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A new strategy has been announced, but performance measures, structure, systems, staffing, leadership behavior, and shared values still support the previous business model. Which tool gives the most complete alignment diagnosis?"
    options:
      - "Peters, Athos and Waterman's 7-S Framework"
      - "Hrebiniak's Model of Strategy Execution"
      - "Kotter's Eight Phases of Change"
      - "Argyris's Double-Loop Learning"
    answer: "Peters, Athos and Waterman's 7-S Framework"
    explanation: "The 7-S Framework diagnoses alignment among Strategy, Structure, Systems, Skills, Staff, Style, and Shared Values. Hrebiniak is also relevant for execution, but 7-S is the clearest broad diagnostic for the seven organizational elements."

    type: select-all
    question: "Which factors belong in the internal side of a SWOT Analysis?"
    options:
      - "A trusted engineering brand"
      - "A new competitor entering the market"

      - "A mature field-response capability"
      - "A change in customer procurement rules"
      - "Limited subscription-sales experience"
    answers:
      - "A trusted engineering brand"
      - "Fragmented service data"
      - "A mature field-response capability"
      - "Limited subscription-sales experience"
    explanation: "Strengths and Weaknesses are internal factors, such as brand, data quality, capabilities, and skills. A new competitor and changing procurement rules are external factors and therefore belong under Opportunities or Threats."

  - id: 10
    type: select-all
    question: "Which statements correctly describe Porter's Value Chain and Value System?"
    options:
      - "It examines activities that create cost and customer value"
      - "It can reveal linkages between activities such as operations and service"
      - "It includes only the company's organizational chart"
      - "It can extend to suppliers, channels, partners, and customers"
      - "It is primarily used to plot market growth against relative market share"
    answers:
      - "It examines activities that create cost and customer value"
      - "It can reveal linkages between activities such as operations and service"
      - "It can extend to suppliers, channels, partners, and customers"
    explanation: "Porter's Value Chain maps discrete activities, not departments alone, and the Value System includes connected activities outside the firm. Market growth and relative share belong to the BCG matrix."

  - id: 11
    type: select-all
    question: "Which factors can make a resource or capability difficult for competitors to imitate in a Resource-Based View analysis?"
    options:
      - "Accumulated tacit knowledge"
      - "Long-standing trusted relationships"
      - "A capability that can be bought from any vendor"
      - "Path-dependent routines developed over time"
      - "Socially complex teamwork and culture"
      - "A generic product feature with no customer value"
    answers:
      - "Accumulated tacit knowledge"
      - "Long-standing trusted relationships"
      - "Path-dependent routines developed over time"
      - "Socially complex teamwork and culture"
    explanation: "RBV considers resources more defensible when they are difficult to copy because they depend on history, tacit knowledge, relationships, social complexity, or integrated routines. Common purchasable inputs and valueless features do not create a durable advantage."

  - id: 12
    type: select-all
    question: "Which observations suggest that a decision is in Stacey's complex zone rather than the simple zone?"
    options:
      - "Cause and effect are clear from proven practice"
      - "Different stakeholders hold legitimate but conflicting views"
      - "Customer behavior must be discovered through experiments"
      - "A standard operating procedure has repeatedly worked"
      - "Outcomes emerge from interactions among customers, technology, and teams"
      - "Safe-to-learn experiments are needed"
    answers:
      - "Different stakeholders hold legitimate but conflicting views"
      - "Customer behavior must be discovered through experiments"
      - "Outcomes emerge from interactions among customers, technology, and teams"
      - "Safe-to-learn experiments are needed"
    explanation: "Complex situations have uncertain cause and effect and incomplete agreement. They require experiments, feedback, sense-making, and adaptation. Simple situations have high certainty and agreement and are better managed through standard procedures."

  - id: 13
    type: fill-blank
    question: "In SWOT Analysis, factors outside the organization that could help it achieve its aims are called ___."
    answer: "Opportunities"
    accept:
      - "opportunity"
    explanation: "Opportunities are external conditions that may benefit the organization. Strengths and Weaknesses are internal, while Threats are external conditions that could harm the organization."

  - id: 14
    type: fill-blank
    question: "In the BCG Growth-Share Matrix, a high-market-growth and high-relative-market-share unit is called a ___."
    answer: "Star"
    accept:
      - "stars"
    explanation: "A Star has high relative market share in a high-growth market. It may need substantial investment to defend its position and can become a Cash Cow if market growth later slows while share remains high."

  - id: 15
    type: fill-blank
    question: "In Burgelman's Strategy Dynamics Model, a rule-abiding company operating in a rule-changing industry environment faces ___ industry change."
    answer: "independent"
    accept:
      - "independent industry change"
    explanation: "Independent industry change occurs when external actors change industry rules while the focal company remains rule-abiding. It is dangerous because the company can remain attached to an outdated basis of competition."

  - id: 21
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "An engineering firm has a low-share service in a mature market. It appears unprofitable, but it provides crucial data and customer access that strengthen a high-growth product line. What is the best interpretation of its BCG position?"
    options:
      - "Automatically discontinue it because all Dogs must be exited"
      - "Keep it only if its strategic synergy is evidenced and its role is explicit"
      - "Classify it as a Star because it supports another service"
      - "Use Ansoff's Growth Grid instead of considering portfolio decisions"
    answer: "Keep it only if its strategic synergy is evidenced and its role is explicit"
    explanation: "A low-share, low-growth unit may appear as a Dog, but BCG should not be applied mechanically. A unit can be retained if it has a proven strategic role, such as supplying data, customer access, capability, or support for another more valuable unit."

    options:
      - "The firm has a fully sustained advantage already"
      - "The firm has an unexploited potential advantage and must organize to capture value"
      - "The capability is not valuable because it is difficult to use"
      - "The firm should use Five Forces instead of changing its internal system"
    answer: "The firm has an unexploited potential advantage and must organize to capture value"
    explanation: "In VRIO logic, a valuable, rare, difficult-to-imitate capability cannot deliver full advantage unless the organization has the structure, systems, incentives, governance, and ownership to capture its value."
