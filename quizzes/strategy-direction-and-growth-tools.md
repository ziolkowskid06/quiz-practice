    type: multiple-choice
    question: "An engineering firm has completed its market diagnosis and now must decide whether to compete through lowest delivered cost, broad differentiation, cost focus, or differentiation focus. Which tool should it use next?"
    options:
      - "Porter's Generic Strategies"
      - "Porter's Five Forces"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
      - "Lewin's Force Field Analysis"
    answer: "Porter's Generic Strategies"
    explanation: "Porter's Generic Strategies selects the basis of competitive advantage and market scope. Five Forces diagnoses industry structure before the choice, BCG prioritizes existing portfolio units, and Lewin maps forces affecting organizational change."

  - id: 2
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A firm wants to decide whether to sell more existing services to current customers, develop a new service for current customers, take an existing service to new markets, or enter new markets with new offerings. Which tool is the best fit?"
    options:
      - "Ansoff's Growth Grid"
      - "BCG Growth-Share Matrix"
      - "Johnson's White Space Model"
      - "Kim and Mauborgne's Four Actions Framework"
    answer: "Ansoff's Growth Grid"
    explanation: "Ansoff's Growth Grid classifies growth options by existing versus new products and existing versus new markets. BCG allocates resources across current portfolio units, White Space examines business-model incompatibility, and Four Actions redesigns the value curve."

  - id: 3
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A product team believes its industry oversupplies costly custom reports and raw data, while underproviding clear decisions, assurance, and actionable recommendations. Which tool should it use to redesign the offer?"
    options:
      - "Kim and Mauborgne's Four Actions Framework"
      - "Porter's Generic Strategies"
      - "Scenario Planning"
      - "Peters, Athos and Waterman's 7-S Framework"
    answer: "Kim and Mauborgne's Four Actions Framework"
    explanation: "The Four Actions Framework challenges industry factors through Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, and Create. It helps redesign the value curve. Generic Strategies chooses competitive position but does not specify how to redesign customer value factors."

  - id: 4
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A stable engineering business has spotted a promising digital service opportunity, but the opportunity needs a new customer value proposition, subscription pricing, new resources, and new processes. It cannot be pursued fairly through the old project-based model. Which tool should it use?"
    options:
      - "Johnson's White Space Model"
      - "Ansoff's Growth Grid"
      - "SWOT Analysis"
      - "Greiner's Growth and Crisis Model"
    answer: "Johnson's White Space Model"
    explanation: "White Space identifies opportunities outside the current business model and designs a new model through customer value proposition, profit formula, key resources, and key processes. Ansoff can label the move as product development or diversification but does not diagnose the new-model requirement."

  - id: 5
    type: multiple-choice
    question: "A company must make investment choices today even though customer adoption and future regulation could each develop in different plausible directions. It needs robust actions and signposts, not one forecast. Which tool should it use?"
    options:
      - "Scenario Planning"
      - "Stacey's Strategy from Complexity"
      - "Balanced Scorecard"
      - "Hrebiniak's Model of Strategy Execution"
    answer: "Scenario Planning"
    explanation: "Scenario Planning uses critical uncertainties to develop plausible futures, stress-test choices, identify no-regret moves, preserve options, and monitor signposts. Stacey assesses the certainty and agreement around a current decision rather than constructing external future scenarios."
